What a raise costs, state by state

Fifty-one sets of rules, eight household shapes, one axis. Each figure is the worst single $1,000 step of earnings in that state’s curve for that household — how much net income falls when pay goes up by a thousand dollars.

    How these numbers were made

    • Every cell is one household shape swept through the PolicyEngine US microsimulation at 2026 rules, earnings varied in $1,000 steps from $0 past 400% of the 2025 poverty guideline for that household size, plus $40,000 of room to recover.
    • The money line is health-adjusted: household net income minus what the household actually pays in health-insurance premiums, net of the marketplace subsidy. Deductibles and copays are not included.
    • Each archetype is a typical renter in the state’s most populous county — HUD FY2026 two-bedroom Fair Market Rent, Census Vintage 2024 county, and the DOL National Database of Childcare Prices centre-based preschool price for that same county, carried to 2026 dollars by the BLS Employment Cost Index.
    • The CCDF child care subsidy is on for this sweep and off for a household’s own lookup. Head Start and housing vouchers are off in both: they are rationed, and assuming a family holds one inflates its numbers.
    • Cliffs deferred to a future renewal — Head Start’s program-year carry-over, a child’s 12 months of continuous Medicaid or CHIP eligibility, a parent’s Transitional Medical Assistance — are counted in the deferred column and are lifted out of every other figure here. They are real losses; they do not land in the year of the raise.

    What the model does not include

    • Immigration status, assets, and household members aged 65 or over are not inputs.
    • Alaska and Hawaii marketplace subsidies are computed upstream against the 48-state poverty guideline rather than their own higher ones (policyengine-us #9482). HotGap does not correct this, so both states’ premium-driven figures are understated.
    • Four state $0-premium wraps (CT, MA, NM, CA) and the child care subsidy in 28 states are local corrections HotGap applies on top of PolicyEngine, pending policyengine-us #9481 and #9405.
    • Massachusetts cash assistance carries a local correction and an approximation notice.

    Where a state’s worst danger zone runs off the top of the axis rather than closing, the safe exit is unknown and the leap is a lower bound. Those cells read past the axis here and must not be charted as a number.
