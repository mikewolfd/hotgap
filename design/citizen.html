A parent with two kids, ages 3 and 7, in El Paso County, Colorado

If your pay goes up, do you keep more?

You are paid $38,000 a year. You keep $67,000. More pay does not add to that until you are paid $86,000.

You keep more than your pay because help and tax money are part of it. This number is your pay, plus help, less what you pay for a health plan.

What ends, and when

  • $37,000

    Your own free state health plan ends. It is called Medicaid. You start paying for a plan.

  • $40,000

    Your kids move off Medicaid. They move to a plan for kids.

  • $54,000

    Food help ends. It is called SNAP. Food help for young kids ends too. It is called WIC. You keep $2,447 less.

  • $55,000

    Child care help ends. The state stops paying part of your day care bill. You keep $9,390 less. This is the biggest drop.

  • $72,000

    Waits Your kids’ health plan ends. It is called CHIP. It does not end that day.

One change waits

At $72,000 your kids stop being able to get CHIP, their health plan. But the law lets kids keep it for a full year at a time. So it ends at their next yearly check, up to 12 months later.

We do not draw it as a drop today, because it is not one.

How common is this pay?

About 4 in 10 parents like you in Colorado are paid $38,000 a year or less. The count could be off by a few thousand dollars either way.

This says how common the pay is. It does not say what you will earn.

Hours

Colorado’s lowest legal pay is $15.16 an hour. Full-time work at that pay is about $31,500 a year.
